Section 1: Executive Summary
Purpose
This document deﬁnes how Crises Control operates as a company.

It aligns Leadership, Marketing, Sales, Customer Success, Product, Engineering and
Partnerships around a shared understanding of:

   •   The category we operate in
   •   The customers we serve
   •   The problems we solve
   •   How the platform creates value
   •   How customers adopt and expand
   •   How the company grows recurring revenue
   •   How Crises Control becomes a category leader

Every employee should be able to read this framework and understand:

   •   What Crises Control does
   •   Why customers buy the platform
   •   Who our ideal customers are
   •   How the product creates value
   •   How their role contributes to growth

Strategic Shift
Historically, Crises Control has often been perceived as a mass notiﬁcation or
emergency communication platform.

While emergency communication remains an important capability and a major
source of inbound demand, it is no longer the primary way we deﬁne the company.

Our customers consistently demonstrate that sending alerts is only one part of the
problem.

The greater challenge is maintaining operational control during incidents.

Organisations need to:

   •   Coordinate multiple teams
   •   Track actions and responsibilities
   •   Maintain visibility of response progress
   •   Document communications and decisions
   •   Demonstrate accountability afterwards

Crises Control therefore positions itself as an:

Operational Incident Coordination Platform
This category reﬂects the broader value delivered by the platform and aligns with
how our most successful customers use the product.

Growth Thesis
Crises Control grows through a land-and-expand model.

Many customers begin with a speciﬁc need, most commonly emergency
notiﬁcations.

Over time they expand into:

   •   Incident coordination
   •   Task execution
   •   Operational visibility
   •   Audit and reporting
   •   AI-assisted incident intelligence

The platform therefore supports customers throughout their operational resilience
journey rather than solving a single communication problem.

Long-Term Vision
Crises Control becomes the platform organisations rely upon to coordinate response
during critical incidents and demonstrate operational control before, during and after
disruption.

The company evolves through four strategic stages:

Alerting → Coordination → Intelligence → Operational Resilience Platform




Section 2: Mission, Vision & Purpose
Mission
To help organisations maintain control during critical incidents through structured
communication, coordination, execution and accountability.

Vision
To become the global platform organisations trust to coordinate operational
response during disruption and prove what happened afterwards.

Purpose
Operational disruption is inevitable.
Poor coordination is optional.
Crises Control exists to ensure organisations can:
    • Activate response quickly
   •   Coordinate teams effectively
    • Execute critical actions
    • Maintain operational visibility
    • Demonstrate accountability
When organisations lose control during incidents, the consequences can include:
    • Financial loss
    • Safety risks
    • Reputational damage
    • Regulatory scrutiny
    • Operational disruption
Crises Control exists to reduce these outcomes.

Core Principles
   •   Simplicity Under Pressure
          o Incidents are stressful.
          o The platform must remain intuitive and usable during high-pressure
             situations.
   •   Operational Focus
          o The platform must solve real operational problems rather than
             becoming a theoretical governance tool.
   •   Accountability
          o Every signiﬁcant communication, action and decision should be
            traceable.
   •   Scalability
          o Customers should be able to start small and expand naturally as their
             requirements mature.
   •   Enterprise Capability Without Enterprise Complexity
          o Crises Control should deliver enterprise-grade outcomes without
             requiring enterprise-scale deployments.



Section 3: Market Category
Existing Market Landscape
Crises Control operates within the broader Critical Event Management ecosystem.

Adjacent categories include:

   •   Emergency Notiﬁcation Systems (ENS)
   •   Mass Notiﬁcation Platforms
   •   Crisis Management Software
   •   Incident Management Software
   •   Business Continuity Platforms
   •   Operational Resilience Platforms

Most vendors focus on one of three areas:

   1. Alerting
   2. Governance
   3. Enterprise-scale crisis management

The Category We Intend To Own
Crises Control aims to own the category:

Operational Incident Coordination Platform

This category sits between:

   •   Alerting platforms
   •   Governance platforms
   •   Enterprise crisis suites

and focuses on real-time operational response.

Category Deﬁnition
An Operational Incident Coordination Platform enables organisations to:

   •   Activate response teams immediately
   •   Coordinate actions across departments
   •   Maintain real-time visibility
   •   Track responsibilities and execution
   •   Record decisions and communications
   •   Generate defensible post-incident reports

The emphasis is not on sending alerts alone.

The emphasis is on maintaining operational control throughout the incident lifecycle.

Category Narrative
Most organisations can send alerts.

Few can manage incidents end-to-end.

Existing tools often support:

   •   Email
   •   Messaging
   •   Monitoring
   •   Ticketing
However, they do not provide structured operational coordination during incidents.

Crises Control structures:

   •   Activation
   •   Communication
   •   Coordination
   •   Execution
   •   Accountability

within a single connected platform.

Strategic Position
Crises Control is not attempting to become:

   •   A pure mass notiﬁcation vendor
   •   A business continuity planning system
   •   A governance framework platform
   •   An IT operations tool

Instead, Crises Control occupies the operational layer where incidents are actively
managed.

   •   Demonstrate accountability
during operational disruption.

Positioning Statement
Crises Control helps organisations maintain operational control during incidents by
combining alerting, coordination, execution, visibility and accountability in one

The Core Problem
Most organisations already possess communication tools.

They have:

   •   Email
   •   Microsoft Teams
   •   Mobile phones
   •   Messaging platforms

The challenge is not communication itself.

The challenge is maintaining structured coordination when incidents occur.

Common failures include:

   •   Fragmented Information
       Different teams operate from different information.
   •   Delayed Decision Making
       Leadership lacks visibility.
   •   Uncoordinated Response
       Multiple teams work independently.
   •   Lack of Visibility
       Critical actions become diﬃcult to track.
   •   Lack of Accountability

These failures create:

   •   Operational risk
   •   Reputational risk
   •   Regulatory risk
   •   Financial risk

   2. Operational Coordination
      Structure communication and response activities.

   4. Proof & Accountability

Most organisations start with alerting.

That is often why they ﬁrst engage with Crises Control.

However the real value emerges as organisations expand into:

   •   Incident coordination
   •   Task execution
   •   Control Centre visibility
   •   Audit and reporting
   •   AI-assisted intelligence

This allows customers to start with a speciﬁc need while growing into a complete

Crises Control is not deﬁned by alerting.

Alerting is the beginning of the story.

Crises Control is deﬁned by helping organisations coordinate response, maintain
visibility, execute actions and prove what happened afterwards.

Section 9: Sales Methodology
Strategic Principle
Crises Control does not sell software features.
Crises Control sells operational control during incidents.
Sales conversations should focus on operational outcomes rather than product
functionality.

The Sales Narrative
Historically, prospects often begin with a communication problem.
Examples:
   • "We need a mass notiﬁcation system."
   • "We need emergency communications."
   • "We need a replacement for AlertMedia."
   • "We need a crisis communication platform."
Sales should not stop at communication.
The objective is to expand the conversation toward coordination, execution, visibility
and accountability.

Four-Stage Discovery Framework
Stage 1: Activation
Questions:
   • How do you currently reach people during incidents?
   • How quickly can you notify critical personnel?
   • How do you know who has responded?
Objective:
Establish the alerting challenge.

Stage 2: Coordination
Questions:
  • How do teams coordinate response?
  • How do different departments share information?
  • Who owns incident decisions?
Objective:
Expose coordination gaps.

Stage 3: Execution
Questions:
   • How are actions assigned?
   • How do you track completion?
   • How do you manage escalation?
Objective:
Expose operational execution weaknesses.

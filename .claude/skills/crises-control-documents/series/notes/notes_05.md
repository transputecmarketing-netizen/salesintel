## Key verified facts
- **Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025 (c. 10):** legislation.gov.uk gives the date as 3 April 2025, and the long title was quoted from the introductory text. The Home Office overarching factsheet confirms Royal Assent on 3 April 2025. It also says the implementation period is expected to be "at least 24 months" and that there is "no legal requirement to comply until the legislation comes into force".
- **Expected commencement:** the SIA's page "Understanding Martyn's Law" (17 July 2026) says "expected that the Act will come into force in spring 2027", with the exact date to be confirmed. Its news story of 4 June 2026 says the dates will be set by statutory instrument. The only commencement SI so far is SI 2026/320 (made 18 March 2026), which brought s.27 (guidance) into force on 10 April 2026. No commencement date had been set as at 9 November 2026.
- **Thresholds:** standard tier is 200 to 799 people, including staff, "reasonably expected... at the same time" from time to time. Enhanced tier is 800 or more. Qualifying events have an 800 threshold.
- **Scope:** statutory guidance (presented 15 April 2026) says "food and drink" "includes bars, pubs, restaurants, cafés and food halls". "Hotels etc" includes hostels and holiday parks. Nightclubs come under entertainment and leisure. The scope factsheet's examples include "a pub with a beer garden" and "a hotel with outside grounds used for dining and events".
- **Standard duty:**
  - Notify the SIA.
  - Have public protection procedures (evacuation, invacuation, lockdown, communication) so far as is reasonably practicable. The definitions are quoted from the factsheet.
  - No physical alterations or equipment are required.
  - There is no statutory training requirement, but staff with responsibilities must know the procedures and their roles.
  - The factsheet's worked example is a 400-seat restaurant.
- **Enhanced duty:**
  - Public protection measures covering monitoring, movement, physical safety and security, and security of information.
  - A compliance document provided to the SIA.
  - A designated senior individual where the responsible person is an organisation.
- **Penalties (regulator factsheet):** up to £10,000 for standard tier, and £18m or 5% of worldwide revenue for enhanced tier. Daily penalties are £500 or £50,000. Restriction notices apply to enhanced tier and events.
- **Notification guidance (14 July 2026):** existing premises must notify within three months of commencement, and report changes or inaccuracies within 28 days.
- **SIA:** compliance is checked by desk-based assessments and inspections, usually with at least 72 hours' notice. Its consultation on guidance ran from 15 April to 12 June 2026.
- **Premises estimates:** the RPC opinion of 22 August 2024 on the impact assessment of 30 July 2024 estimates about 154,600 standard tier and 24,300 enhanced tier premises. It says "the large majority of these premises are in the retail and hospitality sectors".
- **Manchester Arena Inquiry Volume 1 (HC 279, 17 June 2021):**
  - Para 1.2: the security arrangements should have prevented or minimised the impact.
  - Paras 1.52 to 1.57: a member of the public raised a concern with a steward who had no radio. A radio holder tried but the channel was busy, and the concern was not escalated. It was raised about 15 minutes before the concert ended.
  - Para 8.27: the Protect Duty.
- **Manchester Arena Inquiry Volume 2 (HC 757-I and II, 3 November 2022):**
  - 149 recommendations (R1 to R149 counted in the text).
  - Para 10.76: the Operation Plato declaration was not communicated to the other services.
  - Para 10.62: GMFRS arrived over two hours after the attack.
  - Para 21.7: plans "were not known by everyone who should have known about them".
  - Para 21.9: single points of failure.
  - Part 16: Showsec's head of security diverted people, and a radio call went out for first aiders.
  - Para 20.149: the public should be educated in first aid.
- **London Bridge and Borough Market PFD report (1 November 2019):** the attack ran from 10.06pm to about 10.16pm. "All eight died within 15 minutes". The response was "generally well-managed". Para 38 records the police evidence on a legal duty for private site owners.
- **Spiking (Home Office report, 19 December 2023):** 6,732 reports from May 2022 to April 2023, 80% in night-time economy venues, and almost half in bars.
- **Crime and Policing Act 2026 s.134 (spiking and harmful substances):** this is prospective, and is not in Commencement Nos 1 to 3 (SI 2026/689 and 2026/960).
- **IHG:** its LSE filing of 6 September 2022, reported by TechCrunch, said unauthorised activity had "significantly disrupted" booking channels and apps.
- **RUN HIDE TELL:** this is Counter Terrorism Policing guidance for members of the public (ProtectUK).

## Round 2 revision (BRIEF2, 10 November 2026)
The playbook grew from 29 to 39 pages, counting the shared pages that are now added automatically. No redesign was made, and no new sourced facts were added, so the sources page is unchanged. All new examples, timings and names are labelled illustrative.

New pages:
- **Part 01:** an escalation workflow ("How an incident climbs from the shift to head office"). It has four levels tied to the severity ladder, and silence for 5 minutes escalates the alert. The timings are illustrative.
- **Part 02:**
  - A swimlane, "One night, three venues and head office". The lanes are the hotel, a bar, a restaurant, the on-call operations manager and the head office crisis team. Its cards show risk points, decisions and monitoring.
  - A shift responsibility matrix (RACI). The roles are Duty manager, Supervisor, Door and security, Front of house, Kitchen and Head office, across 11 activities.
  - A guest communication table. It covers public areas, bedrooms, guests who do not speak English, guests who need help, outdoor areas, and what never to say.
  - An outstanding actions tracker ("Sunday, 11:00"), with 9 rows.
- **Part 03:** two fill-in templates, a shift safety briefing record and an area sweep and head count log.
- **New Part 04, "Rehearse and review":**
  - The existing scenarios, now rewritten around where it goes wrong, who decides and how it is monitored.
  - A 90-minute tabletop exercise with 6 injects.
  - A post-incident review template.
  - The existing leadership questions.
- **Platform rows:** rewritten as 7 lifecycle outcomes (SOS to CRAiG) under the title "How the modules work together in a hospitality incident". The name "Audit & Reporting" is used. CRAiG is described only as helping users find information in the incident.

Existing pages kept: the first hour (its text now covers the failure points, tracking replies by area and the hand-off to the tracker), the roles steps, the comms flow, other emergencies, messages, the festive steps, the plan, the checklist and the glossary.

### New regional facts verified (primary sources)
- **US, OSHA 29 CFR 1910.38** (osha.gov): EAP required where an OSHA standard requires one; written, oral allowed with 10 or fewer employees; minimum elements (reporting, evacuation, critical operations, accounting for employees, rescue/medical, contacts); alarm system; review on assignment, role change, plan change.
- **US, CISA "Mass Gatherings: Security Awareness for Soft Targets and Crowded Places" action guide** (PDF read in full): Connect, Plan, Train, Report; PA scripts; train staff on "Run, Hide, Fight". Undated; guidance.
- **US, SAFETY Act** (safetyact.gov benefits page): liability protections for products, services, software; Designation caps liability at insurance set by DHS; Certification adds Government Contractor Defense. safetyact.gov listing shows stadium and arena operators among approvals. Phrased as "by application".
- **EU, SWD(2019) 140 final, 20 March 2019** (full text read): implements the October 2017 Action Plan COM(2017) 612 (18.10.2017); Operators Forum included hospitality; good practices: vulnerability assessments, security plan, appoint and train a security lead plus backup, crisis management plan "to alert, confine and/or evacuate" with spokesperson and key messages, smartphone apps for staff communication. Described as reference good practices (guidance).
- **EU, OSH Framework Directive 89/391/EEC** (EU-OSHA summary page): employers take necessary measures for first aid, fire-fighting, evacuation of workers. EUR-Lex itself would not load from this environment.
- **UK, MHSWR 1999 reg 8** (legislation.gov.uk): procedures for serious and imminent danger; nominate competent persons for evacuation.
- **Dubai, SIRA Administrative Resolution No. 13 of 2025, Preventive Systems Manual** (issued 14 April 2025, in force on publication): luxury hotels need a surveillance room with all means of communication with guards, hotel officials, emergency, rescue and ambulance services; 24-hour guards per risk analysis; security operations manager or supervisor around the clock. (Earlier SIRA hotel requirements v4.0, 14 Feb 2022, similar.)
- **Saudi Arabia, Ministry of Tourism, Tourism Accommodation Facility Regulations services guide (V.1 2022)**: licence requires a valid Civil Defense permit and connection to the Shomoos platform.
- **UAE, AE/SCNS/NCEMA 7000:2021** (ENEC-hosted copy): "applicable to all UAE entities"; organisations must endeavour to continue essential operations within predefined minimum levels.

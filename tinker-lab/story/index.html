Curiosity is easy.
The first circuit is not.

Tinker Lab exists because of the gap between wondering how something works and having a circuit on your desk that does it. This is a short story, and an honest one.

The gap.
Small obstacles, one after another.

Ask a child how a robot knows where the wall is and they will listen for as long as you can keep explaining. Hand them a bag of parts and a forty-page datasheet and the questions stop.

Between "I wonder how that works" and an LED that actually blinks there are lots of small obstacles. Which pin. Which resistor. Why the part gets warm. Why nothing happens at all. Each one is easy once someone shows you. Without someone to show you, each one is a good reason to give up.

We wanted a place that does the showing. You can see the current move, press the button, read the line of code that reacts, and get things wrong without anything getting hot.

What inspired it.
Three things that already work.

Paper assembly manualsOne step per picture, nothing left out.

The best instructions are often printed ones: a numbered drawing, a short line of text, then the next step. They never assume you know the trick. Our build guides try to work the same way, and they print cleanly.

Maker spacesA bench, the tools, and someone to ask.

A good maker space lets you try things before you own everything, and someone nearby has usually made the same mistake. We tried to put some of that into a browser: the tools are laid out, and each lesson points out the mistakes people tend to make.

The first blinking LEDA small light that changes how you see things.

When an LED you wired blinks because of code you wrote, electronics stops being magic and becomes something you can do. That moment is why this exists, and it is one of the very first lessons.

So we built the place we wanted to start in.

A workbench you open in a browser, with a lesson for every part on it. Today there are lessons in tracks, from what voltage is to running a neural network on a small computer.

It is still growing, and some of it will be rough at the edges. When you find a rough edge, please tell us.

An honest note

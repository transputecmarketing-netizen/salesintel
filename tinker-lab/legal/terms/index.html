    Safety with real hardware

    Lessons are designed around low-voltage parts, typically powered by USB or small batteries. When you build anything for real:

    • Never connect a project to mains electricity (wall sockets) unless you are qualified to do so. Mains voltage can kill. Use ready-made, certified modules for anything that switches mains power.
    • Check polarity and voltage before connecting power. A 3.3 V board can be damaged by 5 V signals.
    • Lithium batteries can overheat or catch fire if shorted, punctured or charged wrongly. Use a proper charger and protected cells.
    • Soldering irons are hot enough to burn. Work in a ventilated space, use a stand, and supervise children.
    • Motors, gears and robot arms can pinch fingers. Keep hands and hair clear when testing.

    You are responsible for how you build and use real circuits. Follow the instructions that come with your parts, and ask an experienced adult when you are unsure.

    Simulations are models

    The 3D bench and simulations are teaching models. They are designed to be accurate enough to learn from, but they are not exact emulations of real hardware, and timing, electrical behaviour and sensor readings are simplified. Boards are stylised illustrations. Always check your own board's documentation and pinout before wiring it. The transparency page lists the main differences.

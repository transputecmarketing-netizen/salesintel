The simulation is a teaching model.
Real hardware is messier, and that is fine.

  • Arduino code is interpreted

    The Arduino runtime interprets Arduino C++ in your browser. It is not the official compiler, and it does not run every program exactly as a real board would.

  • Python lessons are modelled

    MicroPython and Python lessons show the real program; the 3D bench follows it step by step as a model of what the board would do.

  • Timing is approximate

    Delays and speeds are close, not exact. Microsecond timing, interrupts under load and PWM frequencies are simplified.

  • Electricity is illustrated

    Current flow, voltages and brightness are drawn to explain, not solved like a circuit simulator. Electrical noise, loose contacts and part tolerances are mostly left out.

  • Sensors behave nicely

    Real sensors drift, need time to warm up, and get confused by sunlight, echoes or a cold draught. Expect noisier readings on your desk.

  • Boards are stylised

    The models are drawn from scratch for teaching. They follow the real layouts closely enough to learn from, but always check your own board's pinout before you connect power.

Made with AI assistance.
Reviewed by people.

Much of Tinker Lab was created with the help of AI tools: lesson text, quiz questions, example programs, the 3D models and the code of this website. People reviewed that work and corrected what was wrong.

Reviewing catches a lot, but not everything. If you find a mistake, a wrong value or code that would not work on a real board, please write to and we will fix it.

AI does not run behind the scenes while you use the site. Nothing you type or build is sent to an AI service.

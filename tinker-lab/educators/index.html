No student data.
No accounts, no analytics, no tracking.

Progress is saved only in the browser on the device being used. We never see it.

Our hosting provider may keep standard server logs, such as IP address and browser type, for security and for a limited time. Apart from those logs, nothing about a student leaves the device, and we do not use the logs to identify anyone.

Honest limits.
Worth knowing before you plan.

  • A model, not the hardware

    The simulation is a teaching model. Real parts add loose wires, noisy readings and the wrong resistor from the drawer. Leave time for that when you move to the bench.

  • No class dashboard

    Without accounts there is no teacher view of results. If you need to see answers, ask students to show you the quiz screen or note their scores.

  • Progress lives on one device

    It does not follow a student to another computer, and on a shared computer the next student sees it. Use separate browser profiles, or clear the data between classes on the cookie page.

  • The 3D bench needs WebGL

    Very old devices or locked-down browsers may not show the 3D view. A quick check on one school device before the lesson saves a lot of time.

Questions teachers ask.

Is it free?

Yes. You can open every lesson without paying and without signing up.

Do students need accounts or email addresses?

No. There are no accounts at all. Students open a link and start.

What age is it for?

We have not set an age. As a rough guide, the tracks in the Start here group suit younger learners with an adult nearby, and the advanced tracks suit secondary school, college and adult learners. You know your class better than we do: try the first lesson of a track yourself and decide.

Do we need any hardware?

No. Every lesson works on screen. When you want to build for real, the build guide lists the exact parts and wiring.

Is the code real?

Yes. Lessons use Arduino C++, MicroPython and Python as you would write them for the board. In the browser, the Arduino code is interpreted and the 3D bench follows along; on a real board you compile and upload it in the usual way.

Does it work on tablets and phones?

The layout works down to phone width and the 3D bench responds to touch. A larger screen is more comfortable for reading and editing code.

Does it work offline?

It needs an internet connection to load. We have not built an offline version.

Is it safe for children to use?

There are no accounts, no chat or comments, no ads and no tracking. Children cannot be contacted through the site. When you move to real parts, keep to low-voltage projects and supervise younger students; our terms include a short safety section.

What the surveys say

Adoption doubled in a year. Confidence did too: 93% of IT leaders say their Microsoft 365 governance is ready for AI. Yet nearly three in ten report AI surfacing data it should not have, and two in three learn about problems only after they happen.1,2

What it means

An assistant that answers from everything a person can open makes old permission mistakes visible at conversation speed: the broad sharing link, the former contractor, the folder nobody owns. The fix is mostly housekeeping. It is also tedious, which is why it gets skipped.

Our view: run the clean-up before the licences, not after the incident.

What goes wrong,
and how often.

Across the past year, 77% of organisations had at least one Microsoft 365 governance incident. Three kinds account for most of what surveyed teams reported.

left former employees or guests with access they should have lost1

hit an audit or compliance gap1

had sensitive content reach the wrong people1

Share of all organisations surveyed. Incidents counted include oversharing, stale access, audit gaps and shadow IT or AI. Self-reported.
Our view

None of these three is new. A leaver who keeps access, a link that was meant for one person, a library nobody reviews: IT teams have lived with them for years because they were hard to see and slow to exploit.

An assistant that searches across everything makes them fast to find. That is the real change Copilot brings.

It does not break the rules.
It follows yours.

Copilot does not need a vulnerability to show someone the wrong file. It needs the wrong person to have access.

1

A person asks Copilot a question

It answers from content that person can already open.

2

It searches the places they can reach

Content across Microsoft 365 that their permissions allow them to open.

3

Whatever is overshared is now findable

A company-wide link, an old guest, a site with broken inheritance.

Why a quick fix is not a fix

Microsoft's Restricted Content Discovery hides chosen SharePoint sites from Copilot, but it is a temporary control. It does not change permissions: anyone with access can still open the content.5

What it costs

Microsoft cautions that overusing it reduces how complete Copilot's answers are. Changes to large sites can take over a week to apply.5

Source: 5 Microsoft Learn, Restricted Content Discovery. The three-step explanation is ours, consistent with ShareGate's finding that leaders expect Copilot's access to reflect appropriate permissions.

Two vulnerabilities,
one lesson.

Both were fixed by Microsoft. Both are useful because they show where governance has to do the work that patching cannot.

EchoLeak · CVE-2025-32711
CVSS 9.3 critical

A zero-click flaw in Microsoft 365 Copilot that, according to its finders at Aim Security, allowed data to be pulled out of Copilot's context with no action from the user. Microsoft fixed it server-side in June 2025. No customer action was needed and there was no evidence of exploitation in the wild.6

Copilot Studio flaw · CVE-2026-21520
CVSS 7.5 high

Researchers at Capsule Security showed that crafted text in a public SharePoint form could steer an agent into reading connected data and emailing it out. Press reports say safety mechanisms flagged the behaviour but the data left anyway. Microsoft assigned the CVE, published on 22 January 2026; the CVE record itself does not mention prompt injection, so the link to this research rests on press coverage. Exploitation was shown only in testing.6

Our view

A patch removes a specific hole. It does not tell you what the assistant could reach, whether a form is public, or whether outbound email is monitored. Those questions belong to the organisation, not the vendor.

Sources: 6 MITRE CVE records for CVE-2025-32711 and CVE-2026-21520; The Hacker News (EchoLeak, finder per press); VentureBeat (Capsule Security, per press). We did not read the finders' own write-ups.

The vendor's own order.
Three phases, nine steps.

From Microsoft's Copilot oversharing blueprint. SharePoint Advanced Management is included with Copilot licences; Purview foundations come with Microsoft 365 E3, with extra features in E5.4

Remediate oversharing

  • Find high-risk sites with Purview data risk assessments and SharePoint Advanced Management
  • Apply interim protection: Restricted Content Discovery and DLP for Copilot
  • Fix access: label sites, remove company-wide and anonymous links, review site access, assign owners

Set up guardrails

  • Secure defaults: restricted access for critical sites, no Anyone links, labels at site creation
  • Auto-labelling, default labels, DLP, insider-risk protection
  • Keep watching: recurring risk assessments and alert reviews

Meet your regulations

  • Assess against AI regulations in Compliance Manager
  • Set audit-log retention and decide what happens to Copilot interactions
  • Archive or retire inactive sites and apply retention policies

05 · A 90-day plan
05 · A 90-day plan

Look, fix, hold.

Our reading of Microsoft's steps for a UK mid-market tenant. Illustrative: adapt it to your estate and licences.

Days 1–30
  • Run the site assessment. Rank sites by sensitivity and audience.
  • List leavers, guests and ownerless sites with live access.
  • Agree what Copilot may and may not draw on.
Play 01
Days 31–60
  • Remove company-wide and anonymous links. Label the sensitive sites.
  • Switch on interim protection for the highest-risk ones.
  • Assign an owner to every site that keeps access.
Play 02
Days 61–90
  • Set secure defaults for new sites and Teams.
  • Start a monthly review of new exposure.
  • Report three numbers to the board: exposed sites, stale access, time to fix.
Play 06
Ask your IT partner
“Which of our sites could Copilot answer from today that we would not want a new starter to read?”
Plays refer to The Resilience Playbook. This plan is illustrative and not Microsoft guidance.

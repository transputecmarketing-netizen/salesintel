What the sources say

The NCSC says current language models do not enforce a security boundary between instructions and data, and that prompt injection may never be fully mitigated. Where such a model can call tools, an injected instruction has the reach of whatever those tools can do.1

What it means

Treat an agent like a very capable, very gullible new starter: give it the least access that does the job, ask a human before anything irreversible, and log what it does.

Our view: the question to ask of any agent is not how smart it is. It is what it can touch, and who is told when it does.

Not a product fix
The NCSC: prompt injection “cannot be fully mitigated with a product or appliance”. It must be designed around.1

What prompt injection is.

Three authorities, one idea: text that arrives as data can be read as an instruction.

NCSC

“Prompt injection is where developers concatenate their own instructions with untrusted content in a single prompt, and then treat the model's response as if there were a robust boundary between what the app asked for and anything in the untrusted content.”1

NIST

“An attack which exploits the concatenation of untrusted input with a prompt constructed by a higher-trust party such as the application designer.”2

OWASP

“It is unclear if there are fool-proof methods of prevention for prompt injection.”3

Indirect

NIST describes indirect injection as an attacker injecting instructions remotely, without interacting with the application directly: through a web page, a document, an email.2

Tools raise the stakes.

A chatbot that is fooled says something wrong. An agent that is fooled does something.

The NCSC on tools

When a system calls tools or APIs based on a model's output, the impact of prompt injection becomes “whatever the worse case scenario would be of giving an attacker direct access to those tools/APIs”.1

A design rule it cites

When a model processes information from a party, its privileges drop to that of that party. A model reading external email should not hold privileged tools.1

From inbox to exfiltration.

Three cases in assistants that read your business data. Two are vendor-reported and single-source; the CVE records show no sign of exploitation in the wild.

EchoLeak · CVE-2025-32711
CVSS 9.3

A zero-click flaw in Microsoft 365 Copilot. A crafted email could, per a research paper, lead to remote data exfiltration by chaining past several protections. Published 11 June 2025; Microsoft fixed it on its servers, so customers had nothing to install. Found by Aim Security, per the CVE record's reference.3,4

Copilot Studio · CVE-2026-21520
CVSS 7.5

Capsule Security reported that text planted in a public SharePoint form could steer an agent into sending data out. The CVE record, published 22 January 2026, describes only an information-disclosure flaw; the link to the research rests on trade press (CSO).3,6

Agentforce · PipeLeak
No CVE

Capsule also reported that a public lead form could push a Salesforce agent to email out records. Salesforce said it had remediated the specific scenario and called the vector configuration-specific. Single source; no Salesforce advisory read.6

Sources: 3 CVE records at cveawg.mitre.org (CISA records: exploitation none). 4 Reddy and Gujral, arXiv 2509.10540. 6 CSO Online, 15 April 2026.

04 · What the guidance says

Contain the damage.
You cannot cure the cause.

The NCSC's aim is to reduce the risk and the impact. If the system cannot tolerate the remaining risk, it may not be a good use case.1

Constrain
Favour deterministic, non-model safeguards that limit what the system can do. Do not rely on filters for phrases like “ignore previous instructions”.
NCSC
Least privilege
Give the application its own narrow tokens and handle sensitive functions in code, not through the model.
OWASP
Ask a human
Require approval for high-risk actions.
OWASP
Treat it as untrusted
Test with the model treated as an untrusted user. Mark external data separately from instructions.
OWASP, NCSC
Watch the calls
Log model inputs and outputs, and tool and API calls, including failures.
NCSC
Block the exits
Use fine-grained permissions and deterministic blocking of known exfiltration channels, with user consent.
Microsoft

Microsoft's guidance is vendor-authored. We read no agency guidance on sandboxing or network isolation and attribute none.

Five questions before you
give an agent hands.

Our synthesis of the NCSC, OWASP and Microsoft guidance above. Use it for any tool that can act on your data.

1
What can it read?
List every mailbox, drive, site and system. Remove what it does not need.
2
What can it do?
Send, delete, pay, publish, run code? Start read-only.
3
Who approves?
Name what needs a human yes, and who gives it.
4
What can leave?
Block links, images and mail to outside addresses unless approved.
5
How will we know?
Log tool calls. Decide who looks, how often, and what triggers a call.
Playbook

These map to play 04, Govern AI use, in The Resilience Playbook, and to the oversharing steps in What Copilot Can See.

Illustrative synthesis; not an official checklist.

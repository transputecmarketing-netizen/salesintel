The Next Ten  ·  How we chose
How we chose
How we chose

Three questions.
Ten topics.

We screened a long list against what we could read at source, not what we could find in a search snippet.

01

Evidence

Is there a primary source we can read in full? Government and regulator data beat vendor surveys; vendor telemetry beats a vendor poll.

02

Fit

Does it connect to something Transputec does and can prove: Microsoft Modern Work and Security, ISO 27001 delivery, Copilot consulting, backup and recovery, device lifecycle.

03

Timing

Is there a news event or a deadline that makes someone read it this quarter?

What we found

Eight topics have a statistic we read on the source page or PDF. Several rest on vendor surveys and are labelled as such. Two are marked Research: one needs Gartner's original, the other needs our own survey, before anyone reads them. Sustainable IT, insurance and supplier risk are parked for a later round.

Our standard

A number goes in a report only if we can name the source, the sample, the date and who paid for it. A claim we could not verify is flagged, softened or left out. A topic without a defensible headline is not published; it is researched first.

The Next Ten  ·  Our standard
Our standard
Our standard

Six rules that protect
the brand.

01

Name the source

Publisher, date, sample, and who paid for it, on the page.

02

Keep studies apart

Two surveys from one vendor are two surveys.

03

Label opinion

Our view is marked as ours.

04

Flag the gaps

If we could not read it, we say so.

05

No invented people

No made-up quotes, names or case studies.

06

Refresh or retire

Old data is dated on the page, then replaced.

These are the rules used on every document in this series, including the six built so far.

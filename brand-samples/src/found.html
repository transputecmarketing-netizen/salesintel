What happened

The PHP2Shell flaws were found by researchers using frontier AI models. Wiz says the code had sat there for more than a decade and that what AI changed was turning the weaknesses into working exploits, in hours. Calif, the other team, warns that no model finds all bugs from simple prompts, and that knowing your target still matters.1,2

What it means

Treat the next critical flaw in old, widely used software as more likely to be found, and to be usable sooner. The NCSC judges that the time between disclosure and exploitation has shrunk to days and that AI will almost certainly shrink it further.10

Our view: the useful response is not a new tool. It is a patch process measured in hours for anything facing the internet.

Keep calibrated
Mandiant says 2025 was not the year breaches were the direct result of AI; most intrusions still come from basic failures.9

Humans steered.
Models did the heavy lifting.

Both teams describe human-led work with AI assistance. Neither describes an autonomous discovery.

Researchers
  • Chose a target they knew well, with a deep history of vulnerabilities
  • Decided where to look and which results mattered
  • Judged what was exploitable and what was noise
  • Coordinated disclosure with the PHP maintainers
AI models
  • Helped understand weaknesses in old code
  • Helped turn them into working exploits
  • Adapted exploits across several applications in hours
  • Scaled the audit: Wiz used three AI tools, Calif general models anyone could use
Calif, in its own words

“No model is simply capable of unearthing ‘all bugs’ with simple prompts. At least for now.”2

Calif adds that it expected to find nothing critical, because every lab had already run AI-assisted audits of PHP. It surfaced the first issue within days.2

History

A related out-of-memory problem was reported in 2020 and rediscovered in a 2024 audit, but exploitation was not proven, so it was not fixed.2 That is the pattern AI changes: a flaw that is known but unproven stops being safe.

Sources: 1 Wiz Research, 21 Oct 2026. 2 Calif PHP2Shell write-up. Both are security vendors.

Four years of precedents.

Nov 2024

Big Sleep's first find

Google's Big Sleep agent finds an exploitable memory flaw in SQLite, fixed the same day, before it reached an official release. Google believes it is the first public example of its kind.5

Jun 2025

A bot tops a bug bounty board

XBOW submits nearly 1,060 vulnerabilities to HackerOne, reviewed by its team before sending, and reaches the top of the US leaderboard.7

Jul 2025

Foiling an attack

Google says Big Sleep, with threat intelligence, found a SQLite flaw (CVE-2025-6965) that was about to be exploited, and cut it off. A self-reported first.5

Aug 2025

DARPA's final

AI systems find 54 of 63 planted flaws and patch 43, and also find 18 real, previously unknown ones.6

Jan 2026

curl closes its bounty

Daniel Stenberg ends curl's bug bounty to remove the incentive for low-quality, AI-generated reports.8

Oct 2026

PHP2Shell

Researchers use frontier models to find and weaponise flaws in PHP, then disclose them. Attacks begin four days before the patch.1,2,3

Sources: 5 Google Project Zero and Google. 6 DARPA. 7 XBOW. 8 The Register. 1–3 as listed. Several of these are vendor claims.

The same tools make
noise at scale.

reports received by curl in a single week in January 2026. None was a real vulnerability.8

“Remove the incentive for people to submit crap and non-well researched reports to us. AI generated or not.”

Daniel Stenberg, curl maintainer, as quoted by The Register. He also accepted that AI can be a fine bug-hunting aid after receiving good AI-assisted reports.8

Why it matters to you

Open-source maintainers are the unpaid backstop for software your business runs. If good reports drown in bad ones, fixes slow down for everyone downstream. Ask your suppliers how they track and fund the open-source components they depend on.

What the NCSC said.

The UK's National Cyber Security Centre assessed the impact of AI on the cyber threat out to 2027.

Frequency and intensity

“AI will almost certainly continue to make elements of cyber intrusion more effective and efficient, leading to an increase in frequency and intensity of cyber threats.”10

Vulnerability research

“The most significant AI cyber development will highly likely come from AI-assisted vulnerability research and exploit development. The time between disclosure and exploitation has shrunk to days and AI will almost certainly reduce this further.”10

A widening divide

“There will almost certainly be a digital divide between systems keeping pace with AI-enabled threats and a large proportion that are more vulnerable.”10

A limit

“Fully automated, end-to-end advanced cyber attacks are unlikely to 2027.”10

Date

The assessment was published on 7 May 2025. We found no 2026 update to it. Its judgements pre-date PHP2Shell and are consistent with it.

Source: 10 NCSC, Impact of AI on cyber threat from now to 2027 (7 May 2025). Government assessment.

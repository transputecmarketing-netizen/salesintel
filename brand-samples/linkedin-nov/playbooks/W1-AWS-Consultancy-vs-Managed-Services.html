Introduction

Two purchases that sound the same.

Most AWS conversations start with a vague request for help. The first job is to decide which kind of help you are buying, because the contract, the team and the measure of success are all different.

Consultancy buys a decision

Advice, design and a defined piece of work that ends with a clear recommendation or a finished change.

A managed service buys delivery

A team that runs the estate every day: monitoring, patching, backups, support and cost reporting against agreed service levels.

Mixing them up is expensive

Buy advice when you needed operations and nothing gets run. Buy operations when you needed a decision and you pay to run the wrong thing well.

Signals

When you need a consultancy

You need a decision, a design or a one-off change, and you have, or will have, people to run what comes after.

You are planning a move

A migration, a re-platform or a new region, and you need a plan the board can sign off.

Something needs a second opinion

A cost, security or resilience review before you commit more budget.

The architecture has drifted

The estate grew project by project and nobody can explain the whole design any more.

A decision is waiting on evidence

Leaders want options, risks and a recommendation, not a running service.
Ask: at the end of this work, what decision will we be able to make that we cannot make today?

Decision or delivery · Week 1 · AWS06 / 12
Signals

When you need a managed service

You know what the estate should look like. What is missing is the capacity to run it well, every day.

Alerts go unanswered

Monitoring exists, but nobody is clearly responsible for acting on it out of hours.

Patching and backups slip

Routine work loses to project work, and the gaps only show during an incident.

Your team is firefighting

Skilled engineers spend their week on tickets instead of the roadmap.

Cost needs watching, not fixing once

Spend creeps back after every clean-up because nobody reviews it each month.
Ask: who will run this at two in the morning, and how will we know it was handled?

Common mistakes

Five ways the purchase goes wrong

Each of these is common, and each is avoidable with one clear conversation before the contract is signed.
01

Expecting a consultancy to run things

The report lands, the team leaves and nobody is responsible for the changes it recommended.
02

Expecting a managed service to set strategy

The estate runs smoothly in the wrong direction because nobody owns the design decisions.
03

Scoping by hours, not outcomes

Time gets used, but the decision or the service level you needed is never defined.
04

No handover or exit plan

Knowledge leaves with the supplier, or switching later becomes painful and slow.
05

Nobody owns the cost

Advice and operations both assume the other side is watching the bill.
Remember
Many organisations need both, in sequence: a consultancy to decide what good looks like, then a managed service to keep it that way.

Decision or delivery · Week 1 · AWS09 / 12
Before you buy

Questions to take into the room

Ask these of any AWS partner. Clear, specific answers are a good sign. Vague ones tell you what you need to know.
Ask a consultancy
?What decision or deliverable will we have at the end?
?Who on your team will do the work, and who will we hand over to?
?How will you leave us able to run what you design?
?What is explicitly out of scope?
Ask a managed service provider
?What are the service levels, and how are they reported?
?Who responds out of hours, and how quickly?
?How do you report and control our AWS cost each month?
?What does exit look like if we part ways?

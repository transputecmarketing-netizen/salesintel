Introduction

A climbing bill is rarely one big mistake.

It is usually many small ones, running quietly where nobody is looking. The good news: they tend to hide in the same five places, and each one can be found with tools already in your AWS account.

Visibility comes first

Before anything can be cut, someone has to see it, name it and know who owns it.

Every cost needs an owner

Spend without an owner is spend nobody will question. Ownership turns a number into a decision.

Finding it once is not enough

Waste returns. The habit of looking every month is what keeps the bill honest.

01

Idle and oversized compute

Instances sized for a peak that never comes, or left running overnight and at weekends.
What it looks like
Low utilisation week after week. Development servers running around the clock. Instance types chosen years ago and never revisited.
Why it happens
Teams size for safety, and nobody is asked to size down once the system settles.
Where to look
AWS Compute Optimizer recommendations, and CPU and memory utilisation in CloudWatch.
What to do first
Schedule non-production to stop out of hours, then rightsize the clearest cases.
Owner
Engineering lead for each workload
Bring this to the monthly review, with a date for each action.

02

Storage nobody deletes

Volumes, snapshots and buckets that outlive the work they were created for.
What it looks like
Unattached EBS volumes. Snapshots from servers long gone. Buckets with everything in the most expensive tier.
Why it happens
Deleting feels risky, keeping feels free, and nobody owns the clean-up.
Where to look
EBS volumes in the available state, the snapshot list sorted by age, and S3 Storage Lens.
What to do first
Agree a retention rule, delete what is clearly orphaned and add lifecycle policies to buckets.
Owner
Platform or infrastructure team
Bring this to the monthly review, with a date for each action.

03

Data on the move

Traffic between zones, regions and the internet that nobody designed to be costly.
What it looks like
Data transfer lines growing faster than the services that cause them. NAT gateways processing more than expected.
Why it happens
Architecture choices made for resilience or convenience, without a view of what moving data costs.
Where to look
Cost Explorer grouped by usage type, filtered to data transfer and NAT gateway charges.
What to do first
Find the top flows, then review VPC endpoints, placement and caching for the biggest ones.
Owner
Solution architect
Bring this to the monthly review, with a date for each action.

04

Forgotten environments

Test, demo and proof-of-concept stacks that outlived the project they were built for.
What it looks like
Resources with no tags, unfamiliar names or a creator who has since left the business.
Why it happens
Spinning up is easy and celebrated. Shutting down is nobody's job.
Where to look
Untagged resources in Tag Editor, and stacks with no named owner or recent activity.
What to do first
Tag or turn off. Anything with no owner after a fair notice period gets switched off.
Owner
Each project or product owner
Bring this to the monthly review, with a date for each action.

05

Commitments that do not fit

Paying on-demand rates for steady workloads, or committing to usage that has since moved.
What it looks like
Steady, predictable workloads with no commitment at all, or Savings Plans that are no longer fully used.
Why it happens
Commitments are bought once, then the estate changes underneath them.
Where to look
Savings Plans and Reserved Instance coverage and utilisation reports in the Billing console.
What to do first
Review coverage and utilisation before buying or renewing anything new.
Owner
Finance with engineering
Bring this to the monthly review, with a date for each action.

Run it yourself

A one-hour cost hunt

Block an hour with one engineer and one person from finance. Work through the five suspects in this order and leave with named owners.
01

Open Cost Explorer and group by service

Note the top services and anything that grew since last month.
02

Check compute utilisation

List instances with consistently low use, and anything non-production running all week.
03

List orphaned storage

Unattached volumes, old snapshots and buckets without lifecycle rules.
04

Look at data transfer lines

Find the biggest flows and ask whether they were designed or accidental.
05

Review untagged resources and commitments

Find what has no owner, then check commitment coverage and utilisation.
06

Assign an owner to every finding

One name per item, with a date to come back to it.
Remember
The goal of the first hour is not savings. It is a list of findings with an owner against each one.

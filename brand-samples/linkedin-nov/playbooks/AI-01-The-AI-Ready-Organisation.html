Foreword

Most organisations are not short of AI. They are short of a plan for it.

AI assistants such as Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini and Microsoft Copilot are now good enough to change how everyday work gets done. Drafting, summarising, analysing, coding and answering questions are no longer experiments. They are capabilities your people can use this afternoon.

Yet in many organisations the picture is uneven. A few enthusiasts get remarkable results, most colleagues try a tool once and drift away, and leaders cannot tell whether any of it is making a difference.

This playbook is the practical middle path. It shows where AI assistants create real value, what good use looks like in each team, and how to roll them out in a way that is safe, measurable and sustainable.

Introduction

The tools are ready. The question is whether you are.

Modern AI assistants can read, write, reason over documents and work with data. The organisations that benefit most are not the ones with the most licences. They are the ones that are clear about where AI helps, who owns it and how it is used.

Start with the work, not the tool

Find the tasks that are frequent, text-heavy and time-consuming. That is where assistants pay back first.

Give it an owner

Someone needs to own adoption, guidance and results, or AI becomes everyone's side project and nobody's priority.

Make safe use the easy use

Approved tools, clear data rules and a simple policy let people move fast without creating risk.

Understanding the tools

A capable colleague with no memory of yesterday

Treat AI output as a strong first draft from a capable colleague, not as a final answer.

The easiest way to think about an AI assistant is as a very well-read colleague who works quickly, never tires and has no context about your business unless you give it. It can draft a proposal in seconds, but it does not know your pricing. It can summarise a contract, but it does not know which clauses your legal team worries about.

That framing explains most of what goes right and wrong. When people give an assistant clear instructions, relevant documents and a defined outcome, results are often excellent. When they ask a vague question and accept the first answer, results are generic at best and wrong at worst.

Modern assistants such as Claude can work with long documents, follow detailed instructions, write and review code, analyse spreadsheets and reason through multi-step problems. Many can now connect to the tools your people already use, such as email, documents and ticketing systems, which is where the biggest gains tend to come from.

What they cannot do is guarantee they are right. Assistants can produce confident answers that are wrong, especially on facts, figures and recent events. Good use always keeps a person accountable for the output.

The four jobs

Four jobs AI assistants do well

Almost every valuable use case is a version of one of these four.

Draft

First versions of emails, proposals, reports, policies and job descriptions, written to your brief.

Digest

Summaries of long documents, meetings, threads and research, with the key points pulled out.

Analyse

Working through data, comparing options, spotting patterns and explaining the reasoning.

Build

Writing and reviewing code, formulas, scripts and automations, with a person checking the result.
If a task involves reading or writing a lot of text, it is a strong candidate for AI assistance.

Use cases

Sales and marketing

Sales

Account research and proposal drafts

The task
Preparing for calls and writing tailored proposals.
How AI helps
Summarises public information about a prospect and drafts proposals from your templates.
What good looks like
Reps spend more time with customers and less time formatting.
Watch out
Check every claim about the prospect. Never paste confidential deal terms into unapproved tools.
Marketing

Content that keeps a consistent voice

The task
Blogs, social posts, emails and campaign variations.
How AI helps
Drafts from a brief and a style guide, then adapts for each channel.
What good looks like
More output, same quality bar, with editors focused on judgement.
Watch out
Fact-check claims and avoid publishing unedited AI text.

Operations, finance and HR

Operations

Process documents and standard operating procedures

The task
Writing and updating procedures nobody has time for.
How AI helps
Turns rough notes or a recorded walkthrough into a clear, structured procedure.

Variance commentary and report narratives

Explaining what changed and why each month.

Drafts commentary from the figures you provide and flags unusual movements.

Faster month-end narratives that finance reviews, not writes from scratch.
Watch out
Numbers come from your systems, never from the model.
HR

Job descriptions, policies and onboarding

The task
Drafting role profiles, policy updates and new starter guides.
How AI helps
Produces consistent first drafts in your tone and structure.
What good looks like
Quicker hiring cycles and clearer policies.
Watch out
Keep personal data out of unapproved tools. Check for biased language.

Principles

Five principles for adoption that lasts

Organisations that get lasting value from AI tend to follow the same rules.
01
Pick approved tools, then make them easy to get
Choose business-grade tools with the right data protections, and remove friction to access them.
02
Teach the craft, not just the buttons
Show people how to brief an assistant, check its work and use it in their own role.
03
Start with champions
Find enthusiasts in every team, give them time, and let them share what works.
04
Keep a person accountable
Every AI-assisted output has a human owner who checks it before it matters.
05
Measure and share results
Track time saved and quality, tell the stories, and expand what works.

The sequence

From pilot to everyday use

A simple rollout sequence that builds evidence before scale.

Choose

Select tools and agree a short acceptable use policy.

Pilot

Run a focused pilot with champions on a handful of high-value use cases.

Prove

Measure results, collect examples and refine the guidance.

Scale

Expand team by team, with training and a named owner in each.

Embed

Build AI into processes and review usage and value every quarter.
Each step earns the right to the next. Skip proof, and scale stalls.

Common pitfalls

Five ways AI rollouts stall

Each is avoidable with a little planning.
01

Licences without training

People try once, get a generic answer, and give up.
02

No approved route

Staff use personal accounts, and data goes where it should not.
03

Pilots that never end

Without success criteria, pilots drift and nothing scales.
04

Nobody owns it

Adoption becomes everyone's side project.
05

Measuring nothing

Without evidence, budgets get cut at the first review.
Remember
The fix for most of these is the same: a named owner, a short policy and a regular review.

Glossary

Words worth knowing

AI assistant
A tool such as Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot that responds to instructions in plain language.
Large language model
The type of AI model behind most assistants, trained on large amounts of text.
Prompt
The instructions and context you give an assistant.
Context
The documents, data and background an assistant can see when it answers.
Hallucination
A confident answer that is wrong or made up.
Connector
An integration that lets an assistant read or act in another tool, such as email or documents.
Agent
An AI system that can take a series of actions towards a goal, not just answer.
Acceptable use policy
The rules for how staff may use AI tools and data.
Champion
A colleague who helps their team adopt AI well.
Human in the loop
A person reviews AI output before it is used or acted on.
